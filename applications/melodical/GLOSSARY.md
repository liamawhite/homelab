# Melodical

Music theory learning in the context of piano and guitar.

## Language

**Key**:
A tonal framework centered on a tonic, with a major or minor character. Its scale provides a reference set of notes; a song in that key can also use notes outside that set.

**Chord inversion**:
An arrangement of a chord in which a chord tone other than the root is the lowest note.
_Avoid_: Voicing as a synonym for inversion

**Voicing**:
A particular arrangement of a chord's notes across pitches, including their spacing and doubling.

**Fretboard fluency**:
The ability to identify and locate notes on the guitar neck without counting from a familiar reference each time.
_Avoid_: Guitar neck memorization

**Practice session**:
A short sequence of exercises that the learner can start with one action, either at an instrument or away from it.

**Exercise**:
A prompt requiring the learner to identify musical relationships or locate notes on a keyboard or fretboard, with feedback on the answer.

**Difficulty**:
The level of challenge of an exercise, including the musical material and the support available when answering.

**Practice mode**:
The rules governing how a practice session runs and ends, independently of its musical topic and instrument.
_Avoid_: Game as a synonym for every practice session

**Hint**:
Optional assistance revealed before an answer, such as note labels or a highlighted reference note. An answer given after a hint is an assisted answer.

**Assisted answer**:
An answer given after using a hint, tracked separately from an unaided answer.

**Topic**:
A musical skill practiced through exercises, such as finding notes in a key, identifying chord inversions, or locating guitar notes.

**Proficiency**:
The learner's demonstrated ability within a topic and instrument, assessed through unaided accuracy and recognition speed.
_Avoid_: Completion as a synonym for proficiency

**Recognition speed**:
The active time needed to answer an exercise without assistance. Interrupted attempts do not establish recognition speed.

**Review**:
Practice that revisits previously encountered material to strengthen recall, with selection informed by past performance and elapsed time.

**Tonic**:
The central note of a key, corresponding to scale degree 1.
_Avoid_: Root when referring to a key rather than a chord

**Chord root**:
The note from which a chord's structure is defined, regardless of its lowest sounding note.
_Avoid_: Bass as a synonym for root

**Scale degree**:
A note's numbered position within a scale, counted from its tonic.

**Roman numeral**:
A label for a chord's role in a key, based on the scale degree of its root. Uppercase indicates major, lowercase minor, and a diminished symbol indicates diminished quality.
